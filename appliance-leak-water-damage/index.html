Appliance Leak Water Damage in Hurst, TX

A failed hose or drain line can soak a floor and the wall behind it in hours. Cabinet and countertop damage is covered on the kitchen water damage page.

A washing machine hose can let go while you are at work, and a dishwasher can drip behind its toe kick for weeks before anyone notices. Appliance leaks start small and then spread under flooring, into walls and down through ceilings. If water is on the floor now, call (817) 623-9267 at any hour, because the line is answered 24/7.

Which appliances leak and why

Washers cause the most trouble. The rubber supply hoses sit under constant pressure and can bulge, crack or pop off the valve. The drain hose can also slip out of the standpipe during a spin cycle and pump a full load of wash water onto the floor.

Dishwashers fail at the door gasket, the inlet valve and the drain connection. Refrigerators leak from the ice maker and dispenser line, usually a thin plastic tube behind the unit. Disposals and under-sink connections drip into the cabinet below, where the kitchen water damage page picks up the story.

Location changes the damage. Laundry rooms and utility closets have little airflow, so a slow leak stays hidden and humid. On a second floor, the water drops through the subfloor and appears as a ceiling stain in the room below. A leaking tank is a separate job, covered under water heater leak cleanup.

Safety in the first hour

Close the small shutoff valve on the wall behind the appliance. If you cannot reach it, close the main water valve for the house. Unplug the appliance only if the floor around the outlet is dry, and stay away from the panel if any water is near it.

Call 911 for fire, a gas smell or anyone in danger. Call the City of Hurst Public Works dispatch line at 817-788-7212 only when the problem is on the city side of the meter. Then call us. Washers are heavy, so use two people and do not drag one across a wet floor.

Water categories for appliance leaks

Restorers sort water into three categories under ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage work. As the EPA summarizes those categories, dishwasher or washing machine overflow is listed as Category 2, meaning significant contamination. Water from a fresh refrigerator line is usually cleaner at the start.

That sorting decides what stays and what goes. Carpet and pad that took Category 2 water are normally removed, while hard floors can be cleaned and dried. Time also lowers the category, so a leak that sat for days is treated more cautiously than one caught in minutes.

How we handle an appliance leak

  1. Confirm the water is offWe check that the valve is closed and the appliance has stopped, since drying a floor that keeps getting wet wastes everyone's time.
  2. Extract standing waterExtraction units pull water from carpet, pad and hard floors until only dampness is left.
  3. Map the wet areaMoisture meters read the floor, baseboards and lower wall. A thermal imaging camera shows temperature differences that hint at wet material, and we confirm those spots with the meter.
  4. Open the hidden spotsBaseboards, toe kicks or a small section of drywall may come off so air can reach the wet cavity.
  5. Dry with air movers and dehumidifiersAir movers push air across the surface while dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the room.
  6. Clean and treatAntimicrobial treatment goes on when the water category calls for it, then we recheck readings until the structure is dry.

We do not repair appliances, hoses or valves. A licensed plumber or an appliance technician fixes the source. Texas Occupations Code Chapter 1301 treats plumbing repair as licensed work, with an exception that lets an owner work on plumbing in their own homestead. Once the source is fixed, we rebuild the finishes the water ruined. The water damage repair page explains that stage.

What changes the cost

  • Whether the water reached a wall, a ceiling or a room below
  • How many drying days and how much equipment the structure needs
  • How much finish work follows, such as flooring, baseboard and paint

Insurance and renters

The Texas Department of Insurance names a broken washer hose as an example of sudden and accidental water damage that most policies cover. Gradual leaks and seepage are usually not covered. TDI also says to move valuable items off the floor and to shut off the water, and that an insurer may deny a claim if you do not protect your property.

Take photos before cleanup and keep the failed hose. Check your policy and ask your insurer what applies. Census QuickFacts lists 58.0 percent of Hurst housing as owner-occupied, so a good share of homes are rented. If you rent, tell your landlord right away, because the carpet and floors usually belong to them. A renters policy may cover your belongings, so check that too.

Mistakes to avoid and ways to prevent the next leak

  • Do not push the appliance back over a wet floor and hope it dries
  • Do not run a household vacuum on standing water
  • Do not ignore a small puddle or a swollen baseboard
  • Replace rubber washer hoses with braided steel ones and look at them each year
  • Close the washer valves when you leave for a long trip
  • Set a drain pan under the washer if it sits on an upper floor
