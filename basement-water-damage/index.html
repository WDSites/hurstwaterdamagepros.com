Basement Water Damage Cleanup in Hurst, TX

Water in a lower level, walkout or below-grade room is a different job from a flat-slab house. This page covers the real cases. For the more common ground-level flooding, see flood damage restoration.

A house on a slab has no basement at all, and we cannot give you a Hurst figure for how many houses have one, because no source we trust says. Still, some homes have a finished lower level, a walkout, a below-grade storage room or a stairwell, and when water gets into them the work is different.

Where Below-Grade Water Comes From

Water below grade has several common paths. Heavy rain can push groundwater through floor joints and wall cracks. A window well can fill and spill through the glass. A sump pump can fail from a power outage, a stuck float or a clogged line, and the pit overflows.

A sewer backup is the most serious. When the main line is overwhelmed, wastewater can rise through the lowest drain in the house, which is often a floor drain or a basement bath. A plumbing supply line can also fail in a lower level, which is clean water but still a big job.

Basement safety before anything else

Do not go into a flooded lower level if water is near outlets, a panel, a furnace or a water heater. Call 911 for fire, a gas smell or anyone in danger, and leave the house if you smell gas.

If the house breaker panel is in the wet area, do not touch it. Call your electric utility or an electrician. Never run a pump or a vacuum off a cord that has been in water.

If sewage is coming up, keep children and pets out and do not wade in. If you think the city main is the problem, call the City of Hurst 24/7 Public Works Dispatch at 817-788-7212.

How We Pump, Dry and Clean a Lower Level

  1. Remove the waterSubmersible pumps and extraction units pull standing water. Removal is done in stages so the structure is not stressed.
  2. Take out wet contents and finishesCarpet, pad, baseboard and the bottom of drywall come out. Wet insulation and paneling go too.
  3. Clean and treatSurfaces are cleaned, and antimicrobial is applied where the water category calls for it. Sewage water means removal of porous materials.
  4. Dry concrete and blockDehumidifiers and air movers pull moisture out slowly. Concrete and block hold water deep, so drying takes longer than for framing.
  5. Check the readingsMoisture meters are compared with a dry reference before anything is rebuilt.

Our process follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration. We do not repair sump pumps, plumbing, drains or exterior drainage. We also do not do waterproofing. A licensed plumber fixes the pump and lines, and a drainage contractor fixes drainage, while we handle the water damage and the rebuild of finishes.

Mold in Wet Lower Levels

Mold needs moisture, and lower levels stay damp. Texas Department of Insurance guidance says mold can start within about a day, so dry quickly. If mold is already visible, mold testing and cleanup follow ANSI/IICRC S520. See our mold remediation page, and ask any mold company to show its license so you can verify it on the TDLR license search.

Insurance Questions for Below-Grade Water

The Texas Department of Insurance guide lists sewer or drain backup as an optional endorsement. That means a standard policy may not cover a backup, so ask your agent whether you have it. A sudden, accidental pipe failure is often covered, but flood is excluded from standard home policies.

Groundwater seepage may fall on either side of that line. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and take photos before cleanup.

Prevention and Common Mistakes

  • Test the sump pump before storm season, and ask a plumber about a battery backup.
  • Keep window wells clear of leaves and debris.
  • Do not store boxes or paper directly on a below-grade floor.
  • Do not run a space heater to dry a wet room. Use proper drying equipment.
  • Do not install new flooring before the concrete has been checked.
