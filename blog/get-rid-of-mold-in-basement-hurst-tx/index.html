How to Get Rid of Mold in a Basement in Hurst, TX

Stop the water first, then remove what the growth sits on. Here is what a homeowner can handle and where Texas rules point to licensed help.

By the Hurst Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

To get rid of mold in a basement, stop the water that feeds it, then remove the materials it is growing on. Under Texas rules, visible mold under 25 contiguous square feet may generally be cleaned by unlicensed people. Bigger areas, or growth that has gotten into porous materials such as carpet and drywall, usually call for a licensed assessor and a licensed remediator.

Why cleaning alone never settles it

Mold is a living growth that needs moisture, food and time. Basements supply food in the form of paper, wood, drywall and dust. The only part you can control cheaply is the moisture, which is why every sound removal plan starts with finding the water.

Scrubbing a stain off concrete while a wall leaks behind it is like mopping beside a running faucet. The surface looks better for a few days, then the same spot darkens again.

A removal sequence a homeowner can follow

  1. Find and fix the waterLook for a plumbing leak, a failed sump pump, a downspout dumping at the wall or condensation on cold pipes. Fix it or have a trade fix it before any cleanup.
  2. Measure the areaPace it out and add up the visible growth. Under 25 contiguous square feet is the Texas line for unlicensed cleanup. If you are close or unsure, treat it as larger.
  3. Suit upUse an N95 or better respirator, gloves that reach past the wrist and sealed eye protection. Wear clothes you can wash right away or throw out.
  4. Limit the spreadClose doors, cover floor vents and shut off the furnace fan. Do not use a household fan to speed drying near the growth, because it blows dust around.
  5. Remove porous materialsBag affected carpet, pad, insulation and ceiling tile in heavy plastic and take them out of the house. Cutting them up inside the room spreads more debris.
  6. Clean hard surfacesWipe concrete, metal and sealed wood with detergent and water, then rinse with clean water. Use damp cloths rather than dry brushing.
  7. Dry completelyLet the area dry with ventilation or a dehumidifier. Material that stays damp invites regrowth.

Protective gear is not optional

Disturbing mold sends dust into the air. Gear keeps it off your skin, out of your eyes and away from your breathing. Anyone with health concerns, or who is asking whether exposure could be an issue, should talk with a physician, since health questions are outside what a restoration company can answer.

Never mix cleaning chemicals, and keep the space ventilated while you work.

The reasoning is simple. Porous materials soak growth in deep, so wiping the surface does not reach it. Hard, sealed materials keep the growth on top where cleaning works.

When the job moves past DIY

Call a licensed professional when growth covers more than 25 contiguous square feet, when it sits in or behind walls, when it follows a sewage backup or when you cannot find the water source. Water that is still present, or drywall that stays damp, also points to water mitigation first.

For larger jobs the Texas process separates two roles. A licensed assessment consultant inspects, writes a protocol with cleanup criteria and later checks the work. A licensed remediator then follows that protocol. Owners or managers of buildings with fewer than 10 dwelling units do not have to be licensed for mold work on that property, but they can still choose to hire help.

  • Ask to see license numbers and verify them on the TDLR public license search.
  • Expect a written scope that names areas, methods and materials to be removed.
  • Expect containment, such as plastic barriers, and controlled air filtration for larger jobs.
  • Ask what cleanup criteria the assessor set and how it will be confirmed.
  • If you have an insurance claim, ask your insurer what documentation it needs.

Timing also matters. Mold that has been wet for a long stretch tends to go deeper into drywall and framing, so waiting rarely makes the job smaller. If you spot growth after a known leak, acting in the first days keeps more material salvageable.

What professionals can and cannot promise

Professionals follow the published ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for mold work. They can remove affected materials, clean what is salvageable, document the result and confirm that moisture has been addressed. They cannot promise mold will never come back, because that depends on the space staying dry afterward.

They also do not fix drainage. If your basement leaks through a wall or floor from outside, you will need a waterproofing or drainage contractor in addition to restoration. We are honest about that. Cleanup that ignores the water source only resets the clock.

Cost depends on factors: how much material must come out, how large the contained area is, how many days of drying are needed and how much rebuild follows. Scope and price are explained before work starts. A call to mold remediation professionals is the place to start when you are not sure which side of the line you are on.
