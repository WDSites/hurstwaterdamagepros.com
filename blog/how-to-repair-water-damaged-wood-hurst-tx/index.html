How to Repair Water Damaged Wood in Hurst, TX Homes

Wet wood can often be saved if it is dry, firm and sound underneath. The order of the steps matters more than the products.

By the Hurst Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

To repair water damaged wood, dry it fully first, then test whether it is still firm. Sound wood that has dried can be sanded, filled and refinished. Wood that is soft, crumbly or still reading wet needs to be cut out and replaced. Filler and paint over damp wood only hide the problem, and the swelling, stains or mold come back.

Start with a test, not a tool

  1. Press with a screwdriverPush a flat screwdriver tip into the wood at the wettest spot. Firm wood resists. Soft wood sinks in or crumbles, and that section is finished.
  2. Check the color and smellDark gray or black patches, a sour smell or fuzzy growth point to moisture that has sat for a long time.
  3. Take a moisture readingA pin or pinless meter compares the damaged wood with a dry piece of the same kind elsewhere in the house.
  4. Look for the sourceA dripping trap, a failed supply line or a roof leak will re-soak any repair. Fix it first.

Keep notes and photos at each step. If an insurance claim is possible, TDI guidance says to photograph damage before repairs and keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them.

Drying has to come before sanding or filler

Wood takes up water into its fibers, so a surface that feels dry can still hold moisture deep inside. Sanding or filling too early locks that moisture in and lets finish peel.

Open cabinet doors, pull off a toe kick, and remove base trim if you can do it without breaking it. Moving air across and behind the wood speeds drying. A dehumidifier helps pull water out of the room air.

Thick pieces can take days. Framing, subfloor and cabinet boxes dry slowly, and a professional water mitigation setup with measured readings is how you know the job is actually finished.

What to do for each kind of wood

Trim and baseboards

Thin trim swells and cups but is cheap to replace. If it is bowed, split at the joints or soft at the bottom edge, swap it out.

Cabinets

Solid wood doors and frames can dry and be refinished. Particleboard boxes swell and crumble, and they rarely recover once saturated.

Subfloor

Plywood can dry in place if it stays firm and flat. Soft, delaminated or stained sections should be cut out and patched.

Framing

Studs and joists are thick and often dry well. Wood that stays soft or shows decay may need sistering or replacement.

Furniture

Solid wood pieces can be dried slowly and refinished. Veneer lifts off and glued joints let go, so those are harder to save.

Sand, fill and refinish sound wood

Once readings match the dry reference and the wood is firm, you can bring the surface back. Work in the order below and let each coat cure before the next.

  • Scrape off loose paint, finish and any raised wood fibers.
  • Sand with coarse paper first, then work toward fine until the surface is smooth.
  • Press wood filler into dents and splits with a putty knife and let it cure fully.
  • Sand the filler flush and wipe the dust away with a damp cloth.
  • Prime stains with a stain-blocking primer before the topcoat.
  • Apply finish in thin coats, matching sheen and color against a hidden spot.

Filler is for cosmetic repair of firm wood. It does not restore strength, so never use it to rebuild a soft stud or a crumbling cabinet frame.

Do not seal in a mold problem

Mold needs moisture. If wood stayed wet for more than a day or two, check behind trim and inside cabinet bases before you paint. Texas mold work follows ANSI/IICRC S520, and a licensed mold professional should handle larger areas.

Small visible growth under 25 contiguous square feet may generally be cleaned by unlicensed people under TDLR rules. For anything bigger, our mold remediation page explains what the process involves.

Replacing a section the right way

When wood fails the screwdriver test, cut back to solid material and stop there. Square cuts landing on a stud or joist give the new piece something to bear on. Use the same species or thickness when you can, and prime all sides and cut ends before installing.

Match the new piece to its neighbors before painting. Slightly different profiles on trim and doors show up in raking light, so test-fit first and caulk the joints last.

Honest limits of wood repair

Restoration can dry wood, remove the part that failed, and rebuild with matching material. It cannot make rotted framing strong again, and it cannot undo warping that has set. It also cannot prevent a future leak, since plumbing and roof repairs are separate trades.

If the damage spreads past the wood into drywall and flooring, a water damage repair scope covers the whole rebuild instead of one board at a time. Homeowners in North Richland Hills and Colleyville can call us too.

Call if the wood is soft, if framing is involved, or if you are unsure what you are looking at. A short description is enough to start.
