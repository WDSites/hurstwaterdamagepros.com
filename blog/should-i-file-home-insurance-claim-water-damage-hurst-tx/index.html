Should I File a Home Insurance Claim for Water Damage in Hurst, TX?

The answer depends on how the water got in, what your policy says and what the repair involves. A decision table below sorts the common cases.

By the Hurst Water Damage Pros team · 2026

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

File when the damage looks sudden and accidental, such as a burst pipe, an overflowing toilet or a broken washer hose, and tell your insurer promptly. Slow leaks and neglect are often excluded, and floods are excluded from standard home policies. Whether a small loss is worth a claim depends on your deductible and your policy, so call your insurer with questions before you decide.

Start with how the water arrived

Insurers sort water losses by cause. The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowners and renters policies cover sudden, accidental water damage. The same guidance says policies usually do not cover gradual leaks or seepage. That single line settles more claim questions than anything else.

The wording in your own policy always wins over a general table. Pull out the declarations page and the water damage sections, and ask your insurer to explain any term you cannot read plainly.

Sudden versus gradual, in real homes

A hardwood floor that cups beside a dishwasher might look like a sudden event. But if the supply valve had been weeping for a long time, the insurer may see it as seepage. What matters is the history of the leak, which is why dated photos and honest notes help.

An AC condensate line that clogs in a hot August can overflow into a ceiling quickly. That feels sudden to the owner, yet an insurer may ask how long the line had been blocked. Neither answer is yours to guess, so describe what you saw and when.

A third case sits in between. Water from a failed roof flashing may soak insulation for weeks before a ceiling stain appears. The insurer will ask when the damage began, and honest dated photos of the first stain are your best answer. Do not backdate anything or guess.

The flood question and the 30-day wait

Flood damage is a separate category. Home policies do not cover floods, and mold from a flood is not covered either, so a separate flood policy is needed. The City of Hurst floodplain page adds that there is a 30-day waiting period before National Flood Insurance Program coverage takes effect.

That wait means a policy bought after storm warnings are already in the forecast will not help with that storm. If you have never checked, ask your agent today how flood coverage works for your address.

Thinking about your deductible

Your deductible is the amount you pay before coverage starts. A small loss that barely clears it may not be worth the paperwork, while a large one almost certainly is. You can ask your insurer how filing might affect your coverage in the future, since policies differ.

Cost does not stay small if water sits. Drying scope, materials and rebuild work all grow with time and square footage. We explain scope and price before work starts, so you can set that number beside your deductible and think clearly.

Ask before you decide, not after

Calling your insurer to ask questions is not the same as filing, but read your policy for how it treats inquiries. Ask what documents they want and whether the loss sounds like something the policy covers.

Some owners skip a claim because the visible damage looks minor. Wet drywall and insulation can hold moisture long after the surface dries, so a loss that looks small on day one can grow. That is a reason to get the drying facts early, not a reason to file or skip.

A short path to deciding

  1. Make it safe and stop the sourceShut off the water, keep power away from wet areas and begin drying. TDI says mold can start within about a day, so do not wait on the paperwork to protect the house.
  2. Document before repairsPhotos and video first, then a list of damaged items. Keep ruined items until the adjuster has seen them.
  3. Read your policyLook at water damage language, exclusions, your deductible and any endorsements.
  4. Report promptlyTell your company as soon as possible. Do not sit on a loss while you debate.
  5. Hold permanent repairsTemporary repairs are fine and receipts matter. Permanent work can wait until the insurer has inspected.

Hidden damage and the reporting window

Some water damage hides behind cabinets, under flooring or inside walls. TDI says hidden water damage usually must be reported within days after you first see it. Once you spot a stain, soft spot or musty smell, treat that moment as the start of the clock and call your insurer.

Hurst Flood Facts for Claim Decisions

The City of Hurst floodplain page says only one inch of water in an average home can cause more than $25,000 in damage. That is the city's statement, not ours, and it shows why even a shallow flood can outrun a small repair budget. Look up whether your policy includes any flood coverage before the next storm.

The same page notes that a 1 percent annual chance storm has about a 26 percent chance of occurring over a 30-year mortgage, and that flood policies usually cover the structure while contents coverage is separate. When you review a flood policy, ask your agent whether contents are included and how the 30-day wait applies.

The National Weather Service Fort Worth notes that spring and fall are typically the wettest periods in North Texas. That is a good time to review your coverage rather than after the first storm arrives.
