Four Layers, Four Different Problems

A carpeted floor is a stack. The surface fibers sit on a woven backing, the backing sits on a foam or fiber cushion called the pad, and the pad sits on the subfloor. A tack strip around the edges holds the carpet tight.

Each layer reacts differently. Fibers dry fast. Pad acts like a sponge and holds water for days. The glue that bonds the backing can let go when wet, which is called delamination, and the carpet then ripples or loses its shape. Wood subfloor and the tack strip can swell, stain or grow mold if they stay damp.

Does the Water Category Decide the Carpet?

Largely, yes. The EPA summarizes the IICRC S500 water categories this way. Category 1 comes from a sanitary source and poses no significant risk on contact. Category 2 has significant contamination and may be a health hazard, and the EPA lists dishwasher or washing machine overflow among its examples. Category 3 is grossly contaminated and includes sewage and river or lake flooding.

CategoryTypical sourceUsual carpet decision
1Supply line, sink overflow, rain through a windowLift and dry in place if caught early, discard the pad
2Dishwasher or washer overflow, toilet backup without solidsOften removed, or deep cleaned only if early and the pad is gone
3Sewage, flood water from a creek or lakeRemoved and discarded, and the subfloor is cleaned and treated

Clean water that sits for days can change category as bacteria build up. That is why timing matters as much as the source. Contaminated carpet is usually removed because the fibers and backing cannot be made reliably clean.

Safety in the First Hour: What to Do Before a Crew Arrives

Shut off the water source if you can reach the valve. Stay out of wet rooms if water is near outlets, power strips or lamps, and switch off power to that room at the panel only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. Call 911 for fire, a gas smell or anyone in danger.

How We Treat Wet Carpet

  1. Extract the waterExtraction units pull water from the carpet and pad while it is still in place.
  2. Lift and remove the padWe release the carpet from a tack strip corner, fold it back and cut out the pad. Pad is cheap to replace and almost never worth saving.
  3. Dry the subfloor and the carpetAir movers blow under the lifted carpet and across the subfloor while dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room.
  4. Read and treatMoisture meters check the subfloor, tack strip and baseboard. Antimicrobial treatment and deodorizing are used where the water category calls for them.
  5. Reinstall or replaceClean carpet is re-stretched over new pad and re-secured, and seams are checked. Carpet that delaminated, shrank or smells is replaced.

Drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it. The drying itself is explained on our structural drying and dehumidification page. Floors that cannot be saved move on to water damage repair.

Why Speed Matters Under Carpet

The Texas Department of Insurance tells homeowners to dry quickly and says mold can start within about a day. Carpet makes that worse because the damp pad and subfloor stay covered and warm. Pulling the pad and moving air under the carpet is the fastest way to stop it.

National Weather Service Fort Worth says July and August are the hottest months and air conditioners run hard. Condensate lines and drain pans near air handlers can leak quietly onto closet and hallway carpet. If carpet near the air handler feels cool and damp, check the line and call a licensed HVAC technician for the source.

Census QuickFacts shows 58.0 percent of Hurst homes are owner-occupied, so many households rent. If you rent, tell your landlord right away, since the carpet is usually theirs. A renters policy may cover your belongings for sudden accidental water damage, so check your policy.

Insurance Basics

The Texas Department of Insurance says most policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and usually not gradual leaks. A burst supply line or a failed washer hose is sudden. A slow drip behind a toilet for months is not. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and take photos before anything is pulled up.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Running only a wet vacuum and leaving the pad behind
  • Setting a household fan on top and walking away
  • Putting furniture back on carpet that feels dry on the surface
  • Keeping soaked pad because the carpet looks fine
  • Cleaning carpet that came from a sewage or flood event
