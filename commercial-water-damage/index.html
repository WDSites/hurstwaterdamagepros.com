Commercial Water Damage Restoration in Hurst, TX

Water extraction, structural drying and written moisture records for commercial buildings. Our process builds on water mitigation and is scoped to the size of your floor.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Water in a commercial building rarely stays in one room. It runs under walls, soaks ceiling tile and wicks into shared partitions, and it disrupts everyone who uses the space. Hurst Water Damage Pros handles the water side of the loss, which means extraction, drying, dehumidification, moisture records and removal of the finishes that cannot stay.

What We Handle and What Belongs to Other Trades

Our work begins once the water is shut off. We remove standing water, open wet materials, run drying equipment and log readings until the structure is dry. A licensed plumber or roofer repairs the pipe, valve or roof that caused the loss, and the owner or property manager decides who makes that call.

We do not promise a start time or a finish date. Call the 24/7 line and we dispatch the same day when crews are available. Scope and price are explained before work starts, so you can plan around what comes next.

Safety in the First Hour

Close the nearest water valve or the main shutoff. Keep people away from wet areas near panels, outlets and light fixtures, and have an electrician make anything doubtful safe before power is restored.

Call 911 for fire, a gas smell or anyone in danger. Call the City of Hurst Public Works dispatch line at 817-788-7212 only for problems on the city side of the meter, such as a main break in the street. Photograph the damage before anything is moved.

How We Dry a Commercial Space

Drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration. The standard covers residential, commercial and institutional buildings, so the same ideas about water categories, drying goals and documentation apply to a large open floor. You can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

  1. Extract and assessExtraction units pull standing water. Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera help locate wet areas. The camera shows surface temperature differences, so it points us to where to measure, and the meter readings confirm.
  2. Remove what cannot dryWet ceiling tile, saturated insulation, soaked carpet and the lower section of wet drywall are cut out selectively. Materials that can dry stay in place.
  3. Dry the structureAir movers push air across wet surfaces and dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air. Plastic containment keeps the wet zone separate from areas still in use.
  4. Document and close outReadings are logged until they match dry reference points. Odor control and antimicrobial treatment are used where the water category calls for them.

What Changes the Cost

  • Square footage that got wet, and how many floors or suites it reached
  • Water category, since dirty water means more removal and cleaning
  • How long materials stayed wet before drying began
  • Days of equipment and how many air movers and dehumidifiers the space needs
  • Selective demolition scope, from ceiling tile only to walls and flooring
  • Containment, access limits and whether work must be phased around occupied areas

Insurance, Leases and Paperwork

Commercial property policies are not the same as homeowner policies, and we cannot tell you what yours covers. In general, sudden and accidental water damage is treated differently from a gradual leak, and flood is often a separate coverage. Check your policy and ask your insurer, including whether business interruption is part of it.

If you lease, your lease usually says who handles the building and who handles contents and improvements. Tell the landlord or property manager promptly. Photos, moisture logs, scope notes and receipts help whoever files the claim.

Mistakes That Make a Business Loss Larger

  • Waiting for an adjuster before starting to dry. Document first, then dry
  • Relying on building air conditioning to dry wet materials, which does not remove enough moisture
  • Throwing out damaged items before photos are taken
  • Closing ceilings or laying new flooring over damp materials
  • Assuming dry-looking ceiling tile or baseboard is dry without a meter reading

If materials stay wet, mold can follow. Mold cleanup follows ANSI/IICRC S520, and our mold remediation page explains that process. For a loss that began as a standing-water emergency, see emergency water removal.
