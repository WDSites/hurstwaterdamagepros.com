Wall and Drywall Water Damage Repair in Hurst, TX

Drywall soaks up water and carries it upward, so the wet area is bigger than the stain. We test, dry or cut out, and finish the wall. Whole-house rebuilds are covered under water damage repair.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

Drywall is gypsum pressed between sheets of paper, and both materials drink water. Once wet, the board softens, the paper wicks moisture upward and the insulation behind it holds the rest. Hurst Water Damage Pros finds out how far the water went, decides what can dry and what must be cut out, and then rebuilds the wall surface.

How Water Moves Through a Wall

Water entering at the floor climbs the paper facing like a wick. A puddle that is two inches deep can leave a wall damp a foot or more above it. Water entering from above, such as a leaking supply line or roof, runs down inside the cavity and soaks the bottom plate.

Behind the board sits insulation, wood framing and sometimes wiring and pipe. Wet batt insulation mats down and holds moisture against the back of the drywall. That is why a wall can feel fine at eye level and still be wet at the baseboard.

Safety Steps Before Anyone Touches the Wall

Shut off the water source if you can. If the wet wall has outlets or switches, turn off that circuit at the panel only if the panel is dry and reachable. Do not poke at wet board near a live outlet.

Call 911 for fire, a gas smell or anyone in danger. The City of Hurst Public Works dispatch line, 817-788-7212, handles only problems on the city side of the meter. Take photos before anything is cut or removed.

Dry in Place or Cut Out

Clean water that was caught early often lets drywall dry in place. The board must still be firm, the paper intact and the readings falling. We drill small inspection holes or lift baseboards so air can reach the cavity, and air movers and dehumidifiers do the work.

Drywall is cut out when it is soft, sagging, stained from repeated wetting, coated with dirty water or showing mold on the back. A flood cut removes the board at a set height above the floor, usually 2 feet and sometimes 4 feet, so we can reach wet insulation and framing. We cut at a stud so the new board has something to screw into.

  1. TestA moisture meter checks the board, studs and bottom plate. A thermal imaging camera can show cooler wet areas, but the meter confirms.
  2. Open the wall where neededBaseboards come off and wet board is cut out. Wet insulation is removed and dirty-water areas are cleaned and treated with antimicrobial.
  3. Dry the cavityAir movers push air through the open cavity and dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the room. Readings are logged until framing matches a dry reference.
  4. Rebuild the surfaceNew board is hung, taped and mudded. The patch is textured to match, primed and painted.

Drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500, Fifth Edition 2021, the published standard for professional water damage restoration. Ask any company whether its crews work to it. The drying side is explained further under structural drying and dehumidification.

Finishing So the Patch Disappears

A patch is only as good as the finish over it. Seams are taped and coated in thin layers, sanded smooth and dusted before texture goes on. Matching an orange peel or knockdown pattern takes practice, and a primer coat keeps the patch from showing as a flat spot.

Paint is the last step, and the whole wall is often painted corner to corner so the color matches. Baseboards go back after the paint dries. If the house has older paint, a spot color match may not blend, so we plan for the full wall.

Mold Behind Walls

Mold needs moisture, and the back of wet drywall is a good place for it. The Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation says visible mold under 25 contiguous square feet may generally be cleaned by unlicensed people. Larger areas need licensed professionals, and you can verify a license on TDLR's license search.

Mold testing and cleanup follow ANSI/IICRC S520. If a cut-out wall shows more than a small patch, stop and call. Our mold remediation page explains how the process works, and health questions belong to your physician.

Wiring, Plumbing and Permits

Wet walls can hide wiring and pipe that need work. The City of Hurst Building Inspections page says each trade pulls its own permit, and electrical and plumbing contractors submit a trade license. If your wall has damaged wiring or pipe, a licensed electrician or plumber handles it before we close the wall.

The city has used the 2021 International Codes and the 2020 National Electrical Code since October 1, 2022. Ask Building Inspections at 817-788-7088 what applies to your rebuild. We do not repair plumbing or electrical sources ourselves.

What Changes the Cost

  • Water category and whether insulation must be replaced
  • Whether the board dries in place or has to be cut out
  • Days of equipment while the cavity dries
  • Texture type, paint scope and whether the whole wall is repainted

Insurance Basics

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. A pipe that burst last night differs from a stain that has grown for months. Check your policy, ask your insurer, and take photos before the wall is opened.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Painting over a stain before the wall is tested
  • Sealing a wall with new board while the cavity is still wet
  • Leaving wet insulation behind new drywall
  • Skipping the source fix because the wall looks dry now
