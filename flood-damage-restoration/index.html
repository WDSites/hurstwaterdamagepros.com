Flood Water in Your Hurst Home

Flood water is different from a broken pipe. Water that comes in from a creek, a street or the ground has crossed soil, drains and yards, so it is treated as contaminated. Under ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration, that puts most flood water in Category 2 or Category 3.

Water categoryTypical sourceWhat the work involves
Category 1Broken supply line or clean appliance lineExtraction, drying with air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, moisture checks
Category 2Washer or dishwasher discharge, some storm waterExtraction, removal of wet porous materials, cleaning, antimicrobial treatment, drying
Category 3Sewage, creek or river flooding, ground waterProtective gear, removal of affected porous materials, disinfection, drying, verified readings

Ask any company whether its crews work to S500. Category 3 jobs cost more because more material has to be removed and discarded, not because the water is harder to pump.

Basement Flooding Cleanup

A flooded basement is a race against time because water settles in the lowest part of the house and soaks everything below the waterline. Carpet, padding, drywall, stored boxes and wood framing all pull moisture up and hold it.

The first job is removing standing water with extraction units, often truck-mounted or portable pumps. Wet carpet and pad usually come out the same day, since they cannot be cleaned well after flood contact. Drywall is cut above the wicking line, and the framing is opened up so it can dry.

Then the drying starts. Air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and sometimes air scrubbers run for several days while moisture meters and thermal imaging cameras track the progress. Drying stops only when the readings reach the dry reference, and those readings are written down.

Pumping a basement does not stop the next flood. If water keeps entering from outside, you need a drainage or waterproofing contractor. Restoration cleans and dries what the water damaged. It is not waterproofing and it is not drainage engineering, and we will say so plainly.

Sewage Backup Cleanup

A sewage backup through a floor drain, tub or toilet is Category 3 water. Stay out of the area, keep children and pets away, and do not touch the water or try to clean it yourself. Sewage can carry contamination that household cleaning will not remove, so protective equipment and proper disposal matter.

First find out whether the problem is in your line or the city main. If several drains back up at once, or neighbors have the same trouble, call the City of Hurst around-the-clock Public Works Dispatch at 817-788-7212. The city runs about 155 miles of wastewater collection lines and 2,932 manholes, and a main-line blockage is theirs to clear.

Once the source is cleared, crews remove the sewage with extraction units, take out porous materials such as carpet, pad and wet drywall, then clean and disinfect hard surfaces. Wood framing is dried and treated. A HEPA air scrubber may run while the job is open.

Do not run the washer, dishwasher or showers while the drains are blocked. Every gallon you add goes to the same low spot. Sewage backups are handled after hours the same as daytime calls.

Crawl Space Water and Whole-House Flooding

Crawl space water often arrives from a broken irrigation backflow device or a heavy storm that pushes water under the house. Standing water under the floor soaks the subfloor, insulation and joists from below, and it goes unnoticed because nobody looks down there. Crews pump the space, remove wet insulation and dry the wood. Pumping is the easy part. The crawl space needs a correction in drainage afterward, and that belongs to a drainage contractor.

Whole-house flooding is the largest scale of this work. It means extraction on every level, selective demolition, days of drying and a rebuild plan that may overlap with a repair. For the rebuild stage, see our water damage repair service. Wet drywall that is left in place can become a mold problem, which our mold remediation team handles, and drying is covered in more depth under water mitigation.

Flood Insurance and the Hurst Floodplain

Check your policy and ask your insurer, because standard homeowners policies do not cover flood. The City of Hurst floodplain page says the same thing. Flood coverage comes from a separate National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP) policy, and that policy has a 30-day waiting period before it takes effect. A policy bought during a storm warning will not help with that storm.

Flood policies usually cover the structure, with contents coverage separate. The City of Hurst floodplain page says only one inch of water in an average home can cause more than $25,000 in damage. It also notes that FEMA has mapped stream flooding in Hurst, but flooding of streets and yards in smaller storms has not been mapped. Call the City Engineering Division at 817-788-7076 for flood map information, or check FEMA's flood map for your address.

If repairs will cost 50 percent or more of the home's pre-damage market value, the city treats it as substantial damage and requires a building permit through Building Inspections at City Hall, 1505 Precinct Line Rd, 817-788-7088. Ask before demolition starts. Also keep debris out of creeks, channels and ditches, because dumping there is illegal in Hurst.

Texas Department of Insurance guidance also says mold from a flood is not covered by a home policy. Take photos or video before cleanup, keep receipts for temporary repairs, and report promptly.

What Affects the Cost of Flood Restoration

Water category

Category 3 water means more material is removed and discarded, with added protective steps.

Affected square footage

More floor area and more levels mean more extraction, more equipment and more days.

Materials involved

Carpet and pad, drywall height, hardwood, insulation and stored contents each change the scope.

Drying days and equipment

Air movers, LGR dehumidifiers and scrubbers run until readings are dry, so slow-drying structures cost more.

Storm Season in Hurst

The National Weather Service in Fort Worth says spring and fall are typically the wettest periods in North Texas, mostly from thunderstorms, and that flooding occurs nearly every year somewhere in the region. Annual rainfall can range from under 20 to over 50 inches. A wet spring is a good time to walk the yard and make sure drainage ditches on your land are clear, as the city asks owners to do.

Hurst also lists retrofit options on its floodplain page, including elevating, dry floodproofing, wet floodproofing and preventing basement flooding from sewer backup or sump pump failure. Those are projects for other trades. We clean up after the water and tell you when the cause needs a different specialist.
