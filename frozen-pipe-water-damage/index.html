Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage in Hurst, TX

A split pipe floods the house when the ice melts, often inside walls and attic spaces you cannot see. Standing water goes out through emergency water removal, then the hidden cavities get dried.

☎ Call (817) 623-9267

A frozen pipe is a problem waiting for the thaw. Ice often seals a crack, so nothing leaks until the temperature climbs and water pours out behind a wall or above a ceiling. If water is showing anywhere, close the main valve and call (817) 623-9267, which is answered 24/7.

Why the damage shows up at thaw time

Water expands as it freezes, and that pressure splits copper, PEX and PVC lines or pops a fitting loose. The split can sit quiet for hours. When the ice melts, the pipe delivers full line pressure through a hole, and gallons run into insulation and drywall before the first stain appears.

The National Weather Service says heavy snow is fairly rare in North Texas, while freezing rain and sleet affect the region more often. That matters because ice storms can last for days and drop the temperature below freezing overnight again and again. Each refreeze and thaw can open a pipe that survived the last one, so keep checking after the first warm afternoon.

Where pipes freeze in a Hurst house

Lines in attics, exterior walls, garages and crawl spaces get the least warmth from the living area. In a region where hard freezes are occasional, those runs often have little insulation, and outdoor faucets and sprinkler lines sit completely exposed. Water heater supply lines in a garage closet are another common spot.

National Weather Service data for DFW Airport shows the average first freeze falls on November 22 and the average last freeze on March 12. Those are airport averages, not Hurst figures. The practical point is that a freeze can arrive earlier or later than the average, so check exposed pipes in November and again in early spring.

Safety in the first hour

Close the main water valve first, then open the lowest faucet to drain the lines. If water is near outlets, light fixtures or the panel, stay out of that room and cut power only if you can reach the panel without stepping in water.

Call 911 for fire, a gas smell or anyone in danger. Call the City of Hurst Public Works Dispatch at 817-788-7212 only if the break is on the city side of the meter. Then call us for the wet house.

How hidden wet cavities get dried

  1. Map the waterMoisture meters check walls, ceilings and floors. A thermal imaging camera shows cooler wet areas behind surfaces, and the meter confirms each one.
  2. Extract what is standingExtraction units pull water from carpet, pad and hard floors, and wet insulation is bagged out.
  3. Open the cavitiesBaseboards come off and small openings are cut so air can reach the studs and the back of the drywall. Soaked drywall or ceiling sections are removed.
  4. Dry with air and dehumidifiersAir movers push dry air across surfaces while dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the room. Attic spaces and wall cavities get their own airflow.
  5. Check daily and sign off on readingsMeter readings are taken until wet areas match dry reference readings from unaffected parts of the house.

We handle the water damage, the drying and the rebuild of finishes. A licensed plumber repairs or replaces the split pipe, and that repair should happen before drying starts. Our work follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

What changes the cost

  • How many rooms and floors the thaw reached, since upstairs leaks run down through lower ceilings.
  • Materials involved, such as drywall, blown insulation, hardwood or carpet.
  • How long the water ran before the main valve was closed.
  • Drying days and the number of air movers and dehumidifiers needed.
  • Rebuild scope, from patching one ceiling to replacing several wall sections.

Scope and price are explained before work starts. We never quote a number without seeing the water first, and a phone call gives you a plain description of what the job involves.

Insurance and frozen pipes

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says most homeowners and renters policies cover sudden, accidental water damage such as a burst pipe. Slow leaks and seepage usually are not covered. Flood is a separate matter and needs its own flood policy.

Check your policy and ask your insurer. Tell your company as soon as possible, take photos or video before repairs, and keep receipts for temporary repairs. TDI also warns that mold can start within about a day, so dry quickly.

Mistakes to avoid and how to prevent a repeat

Do not thaw a pipe with an open flame, and do not heat the house with an oven or gas stovetop. A City of Hurst winter weather notice from January 2026 tells residents to let faucets trickle, open cabinet doors under sinks so warm air reaches the pipes, and insulate outdoor pipes. It specifically warns against heating a home with an oven. Do those steps before the freeze arrives, not after.

The National Weather Service says DFW stayed at or below freezing for about 139 hours, nearly six days, in February 2021. A freeze that long can split pipes in several places at once, so walk the whole house once the water is back on. TDI advises insulating exposed pipes starting in the attic and wrapping outdoor faucets before a freeze.
