A kitchen is packed with water lines, drains, appliances and layered materials. A small leak under the sink can soak a cabinet base, run under the flooring and reach the room next door. Kitchen water damage restoration is mostly about finding where that water went and drying every layer it touched.

Where Water Hides in a Kitchen

Water moves along the lowest path it finds. In a kitchen, that is usually the gap behind the toe kick, the space under the cabinet boxes and the seams of the floor. The surface can look dry while the cabinet base, the subfloor and the bottom of the wall are still wet.

Cabinet material makes a big difference. Plywood boxes tolerate a brief wetting and often dry well. Particleboard swells when it soaks, loses strength and usually cannot be brought back. Solid wood face frames are the most forgiving of the three.

Safety first in a wet kitchen

Kitchens put outlets, a range and appliances close to water. Do not stand in water and touch an outlet, a cord or an appliance. Turn off the breaker for the kitchen only if you can reach the panel while dry, and call an electrician if you are unsure.

Shut off the supply valve under the sink, or the main water valve for the house, if you can do it safely. Call 911 for fire, a gas smell or anyone hurt, and leave the house if you smell gas.

If water seems to be coming from the street, the meter or the city line, call the City of Hurst 24/7 Public Works Dispatch at 817-788-7212.

Clean Water, Gray Water and Why It Matters

The EPA summarizes the IICRC S500 water categories in a report on flood-related cleaning. Category 1 comes from a sanitary source, such as a supply line, with no significant risk from contact. Category 2 has significant contamination, and EPA lists dishwasher or washing machine overflow among its examples. Category 3 is grossly contaminated and includes sewage.

In the kitchen, that means a snapped supply line and a dishwasher drain failure are not the same job. Category 1 water that has been sitting for days can also get worse. The category sets what is dried in place and what is removed, so crews decide it on site rather than by guess.

How We Dry Kitchen Cabinets and Floors

  1. Extract and mapExtraction units pull standing water. Moisture meters and, where useful, a thermal imaging camera show wet areas behind finished surfaces. The camera only shows temperature differences, so the meter readings confirm.
  2. Open the cavityWe remove the toe kick so air can reach the space under the cabinets, and drill small holes or remove a panel where trapped air needs a path.
  3. Dry with air and dehumidifiersAir movers push air across surfaces. Dehumidifiers pull the moisture out of the air. Some floors are dried with injection systems that push dry air under the covering.
  4. Remove what cannot be savedSwollen particleboard, soaked insulation and floors that will not dry in place come out.
  5. Confirm dry, then rebuildReadings are compared with a dry reference before cabinets, floors and finishes go back.

Drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration. A licensed plumber repairs the leaking line, drain or faucet. We do not repair plumbing or appliances. We dry the kitchen and rebuild the finishes afterward, as described under water damage repair.

Cabinets, Counters and Floors: Save or Replace?

Counters are rarely the problem, since stone, quartz and laminate tops are not harmed by water. The problem is the cabinet box under them. If the base is particleboard and has swollen, the run often has to be replaced, and the counter comes off with it.

The Census reports a median owner-occupied home value of $315,200 in Hurst. For a house at that value, a full cabinet run can be a large part of a repair budget, which is why a careful dry-out of a salvageable cabinet is worth the effort. Wall board behind the cabinets is handled the way we describe on our drywall water damage repair page.

What Changes the Cost

  • Water category and how long the water sat before drying began.
  • Cabinet material, and whether the boxes can be dried or must be replaced.
  • Floor type and layers, since tile over backer board dries differently from hardwood.
  • Square footage affected, including the ceiling below a second-floor kitchen.
  • Drying days, equipment and the rebuild scope. Scope and price are explained before work starts.

Insurance for Kitchen Leaks

The Texas Department of Insurance says most homeowners and renters policies cover sudden, accidental water damage, with a toilet overflow or broken washer hose as typical examples. Gradual leaks and seepage are usually not covered. Check your policy and ask your insurer how your loss is classified.

TDI advises shutting off the water source, drying quickly because mold can start within about a day, and taking photos or video before repairs. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them.

Mistakes That Make Kitchens Worse

  • Leaving the toe kick on, which traps moisture under the cabinets.
  • Putting new flooring over a damp subfloor.
  • Running a space heater or hair dryer on cabinets, which can warp the material.
  • Using a wet-dry vacuum on water near outlets or appliance cords.
