How rain finds its way in

Most roof leaks start at a weak point rather than in the middle of a shingle field. Common entry spots are flashing around chimneys and walls, the seals on plumbing vents, valleys where two slopes meet, and shingles lifted or cracked by wind or hail. Nail pops and worn sealant on skylights also let water through.

The National Weather Service Fort Worth says large hail and damaging winds occur nearly every year somewhere in North Texas, and severe weather peaks in spring. For a Hurst homeowner, that means a roof can be bruised by one spring storm and only start leaking months later. Check the ceiling after any hard storm, even when nothing dripped that night.

First hour safety

Move people and belongings out from under a sagging ceiling, because wet drywall is heavy and can drop. Do not use a ceiling fixture that is dripping. Shut off power to that circuit at the panel only if you can reach it without standing in water.

Call 911 for fire, a gas smell or anyone in danger. A roof leak is not a city utility problem, so Public Works Dispatch is not the number for this one. Call a roofer for the roof and call us for the water.

Our process after a roof leak

  1. Find the wet areaMoisture meters check the ceiling, the walls below and the attic floor. A thermal imaging camera shows cooler wet patches, and the meter confirms them.
  2. Remove what cannot drySoaked attic insulation and sagging ceiling drywall are cut out and bagged so they stop holding water against the framing.
  3. Dry the framing and deckingAir movers and dehumidifiers dry rafters, joists and the underside of the roof decking. Airflow is placed so it reaches the attic as well as the room below.
  4. Treat and deodorizeDamp wood and cavities are treated with antimicrobial where the water has sat, and musty odors are handled before anything is closed up.
  5. Monitor and rebuildReadings are taken until the materials match dry reference levels. Then the ceiling is patched, textured, primed and painted.

The roof itself is not our work. A licensed roofer finds and repairs the leak, and ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration, assumes the property owner finds and fixes the source. You can ask any company whether its crews work to that standard.

Single leak or storm damage

This page covers one leak or one slow stain. When wind, hail or a fallen limb opened several spots at once, the work is bigger and is described on the storm water damage page. Wet drywall below the leak follows the steps in drywall water damage repair.

The IICRC issued a position statement in February 2026 saying water from weather events, such as wind-driven rain or entry from above, is not automatically Category 3. The category is based on what crews find at the property. For you, that means clean rain through a roof is often treated differently from sewage, though old attic debris and insulation can add contamination.

What changes the cost

  • How much insulation and ceiling area got wet.
  • How long the leak went on, since older leaks reach more material.
  • Whether the water stayed clean or picked up attic debris.
  • Attic access and ceiling height, which affect how equipment is set up.
  • Drying days and the rebuild scope for the ceiling and paint.

Scope and price are explained before work starts, and no number is quoted without knowing what is wet.

Insurance and roof leaks

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says sudden and accidental water damage is often covered, while gradual leaks and seepage generally are not. A leak that opens during a storm is treated differently from a stain that has grown for months. Check your policy and ask your insurer.

Tell your company as soon as possible and photograph the ceiling and attic before anything is touched. Make only temporary repairs, keep receipts and keep damaged material until the adjuster has seen it.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not climb onto a wet roof, and do not paint over a stain. Paint hides the problem and traps moisture, and the leak will bleed back through. If the ceiling is wet more than a day, mold can start, which is handled under mold remediation.

Before a roofer starts a replacement, ask whether a permit applies. Hurst Building Inspections, at 817-788-7088, lists roof contractor documents and a certificate of final roof inspection among its permit paperwork. A call to the city tells you what applies to your address. For homes near North Richland Hills or Colleyville, ask that city's building department instead.
