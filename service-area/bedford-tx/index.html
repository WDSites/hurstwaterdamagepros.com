Who runs the water and sewer in Bedford

The City of Bedford operates its own water and sewer. Public Works is at 1813 Reliance Pkwy, and staff are on call around the clock. The city maintains roughly 212.5 miles of water line, 170.3 miles of sewer line and 2,815 manholes. Water comes from the Trinity River Authority, and wastewater is treated by TRA's Central Regional system under a contract dating from 1973.

The dividing line is practical. Water bubbling up in the street or a sewer overflowing in the right of way is a call to Public Works. Water inside your walls, ceiling or floors is ours. If you are not sure, call us and we will help you work out which side of the meter the problem sits on.

Electrical and sewage hazards

Water and electricity do not mix. If water reaches outlets, appliances or the panel, stay out of the wet area and have the power shut off by a licensed electrician before anyone steps in.

Sewage is contaminated. Keep children and pets away, do not wade through it, and do not run fans through it.

Storm drains, creeks and flood coverage in Bedford

Every creek channel in Bedford drains to the Trinity River, and the city notes that debris can clog storm drain inlets. In a heavy spring storm, a blocked inlet sends water into yards and toward garages. Keeping leaves and trash away from the inlet in front of your house is a small job that matters, and a clogged one should be reported to the city.

Whether your address sits in a mapped flood zone is a separate question, so check the FEMA flood map for your address. Standard homeowners policies do not cover flood damage, and Texas Department of Insurance guidance says a separate flood policy is needed.

Insurance, permits and what comes next

Most homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage such as a burst pipe or broken washer hose. They usually do not cover gradual leaks or seepage. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and report hidden damage promptly because Texas Department of Insurance says it generally must be reported within days of first seeing it.

Larger rebuilds may need a permit. Ask the city building department, which is at 1805 L Don Dodson Dr in Bedford. Permit questions in Euless or North Richland Hills go to those cities instead.
