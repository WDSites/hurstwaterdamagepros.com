Bellaire is in the southeast part of Hurst, ZIP 76053, and it is one of the few neighborhood names the City itself uses in its own documents. When water comes through a ceiling or across a floor here, the first question is often who is responsible for stopping it. This page covers houses and apartments, and gives renters their own section.

Where Bellaire Sits in Hurst

A City of Hurst profile page names the Bellaire Subdivision as the original development. The Bellaire Sustainable Development Plan, a City document prepared with NCTCOG in 2008, treats Bellaire as a study area in the southeast corner of Hurst. That study area takes in the Pipeline Road corridor and the Bellaire Shopping Center.

Bellaire Elementary is at 501 Bellaire Dr, and Vivagene Copeland Park is at 501 Pecan Dr. Listing sites show a mix of houses and apartment communities along Bellaire Dr and E Pipeline Rd. Treat all of this as approximate, because we have not seen an official boundary map.

That mix changes how a leak plays out. In a house, the owner usually stops the water and starts the repair. In an apartment, the water may come from a neighbor's unit or from a shared line, and the property manager controls who works on the building.

Renters: What to Do and Who to Tell

If you rent in Bellaire, your lease and your landlord shape the next hour. Work through these steps and note the date and time of each.

  1. Stop the water if you safely canClose the valve under the sink or behind the toilet. If you cannot reach it, tell the property manager right away.
  2. Report it to your landlord or property manager firstUse the maintenance contact in your lease. Say where the water is and what is wet, and keep a copy of what you sent.
  3. Document your own belongingsPhotograph furniture, electronics, clothing and rugs before moving them, and list item names.
  4. Ask your renters policy questionTexas Department of Insurance guidance says most renters policies cover sudden, accidental water damage. Ask your insurer what applies.
  5. Ask who is arranging the dryingIn a managed building the owner usually decides who dries the structure. Keep damaged items and receipts until an adjuster has seen them.

Flood Questions for Bellaire

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. The City of Hurst floodplain page says FEMA has mapped stream flooding, while localized street and yard flooding is not mapped. The City Floodplain Administrator at 817-788-7076 can give you flood map information for a specific address.

Water damage inside a building can come from plumbing, appliances, roofs and storms as well as from mapped floods. Homeowner and renters policies generally do not cover flood, so ask your insurer about flood coverage separately.

Water Damage Safety Before Help Arrives

Safety first

Keep people and pets out of standing or contaminated water. Do not touch electrical items or breakers while you are standing in water.

Shut off the water at the main only if it is safe and you can reach it. Photograph the damage when it is safe to do so.

Call 911 for fire, gas, electrical danger or anyone in danger. For a main-line water problem, call the utility. In Hurst that is Public Works Dispatch at 817-788-7212.

Sewage or other contaminated water needs professional handling. Fans and a household vacuum are not enough.

What Extraction and Drying Involve

Extraction

Pumps and extractors pull standing water out of carpet and floors.

Moisture meters

Readings in drywall, subfloor and framing show what is wet.

Air movers

Fans push air across wet surfaces to pull moisture out.

Dehumidifiers

They remove moisture from the air so it does not settle back in.

The published standard for this work is ANSI/IICRC S500. You can ask any company whether its work follows it. For deeper wall and floor problems, our structural drying page explains how readings are tracked until the structure is ready.

Insurance Documentation

Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope help your insurer see what happened. The Texas Department of Insurance says sudden, accidental damage is usually covered, while gradual leaks and floods usually are not. Coverage depends on the cause and your policy.

Talk to your insurer before permanent repairs. We cannot promise coverage. Scope and price are explained before work starts, so you can share them with your insurer or property manager.
