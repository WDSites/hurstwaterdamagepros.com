The Euless water year, season by season

The National Weather Service in Fort Worth says spring and fall are typically the wettest periods in this region, mostly from thunderstorms, and that severe weather peaks in spring. Hail, damaging wind and flooding show up somewhere in North Texas nearly every year. For a Euless homeowner that means checking gutters, downspouts and door thresholds before April, because water that reaches a threshold goes straight into flooring.

July and August bring highs in the 90s and often 100 or more, so air conditioners run almost nonstop. Condensate lines clog, drain pans overflow and attic units drip onto ceilings below. A stained ceiling in summer is often an AC problem, not a roof problem, so look at the unit first. Our water damage cleanup work covers these ceiling and wall leaks.

For freeze months, Texas Department of Insurance advice is to know your shutoff valves, insulate exposed pipes starting in the attic and wrap outdoor faucets. The average freeze-free stretch runs from March to November, so a freeze outside it can split a pipe.

Where Euless water comes from and who to call

Euless takes its primary supply from the Trinity River Authority, which draws on the Cedar Creek and Richland Chambers reservoirs, and it also uses city wells in the Trinity Aquifer. The Public Works Utility Division provides 24 hour service. If a water main is leaking in the street or your meter box is flooding, call the city's utility division. Water inside your home is ours.

Electrical and sewage safety

Water and electricity do not mix. If water reaches outlets, appliances or a breaker panel, stay out and have the power cut at the main only if you can reach it dry. Never wade through standing water near live wiring.

Sewage and drain backup carries bacteria. Keep children, pets and bare skin away, and do not run fans through it. Call us and let trained crews contain it.

Insurance and permits in Euless

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says most homeowners and renters policies cover sudden, accidental water damage like a burst pipe or broken washer hose, and usually do not cover gradual leaks. Flood damage is excluded from standard home policies and needs a separate flood policy. Check your policy, ask your insurer and report promptly, because hidden damage generally must be reported within days after you first see it.

If repairs after the water is out are large, ask the city building department whether a permit applies. Mold that starts after wet materials sit is a separate matter, and our mold remediation page explains it. For a pipe or fixture break, water mitigation is the fast first step.
