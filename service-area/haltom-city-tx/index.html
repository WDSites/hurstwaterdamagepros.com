When creek water or flooding reaches a Haltom City home

Creek water is not clean water. It carries mud, yard chemicals and sewage, so carpet, pad and drywall touched by it usually come out rather than dry in place. The first job is pumping out standing water and removing what cannot be saved. Then we dry the structure to measured targets, following the published ANSI/IICRC S500 standard.

Floodplain and rating facts for Haltom City

The city holds a Class 8 rating in the National Flood Insurance Program, which shows it takes part in the program. That does not tell you whether your home is in a mapped flood zone. Check the FEMA flood map for your address and ask the city, then ask your insurer about separate flood coverage.

Flood policies generally take time to start, so do not wait for a storm forecast to look into one. Texas Department of Insurance says a standard home policy excludes flood damage, and mold caused by flooding is not covered either. Sewer lines, city Public Works and what belongs to you

Haltom City runs its water and sewer system from Public Works at 4200 Hollis St. Call the city's published Public Works number for a street-side or main-line problem. We are not quoting an after-hours number here because the numbers listed for the city conflict, so confirm with the city directly.

Under the city's Sanitary Sewer Policy, the sewer lateral belongs to the property owner. That is the pipe from your house to the main. If sewage backs up inside your home, the cleanup is our call, and a plumber or the city can tell you whether the stoppage is on your lateral or in the main. Fort Worth Water also lists Haltom City as a customer city for a Big Fossil Creek sewer main project.
