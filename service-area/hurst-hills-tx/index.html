Burst Pipes Show Up After the Thaw

Pipes in outside walls

Water lines in exterior walls and attics lose heat first. A split may not leak until the ice melts.

Outdoor faucets

Unwrapped spigots can crack, and the water runs into the wall behind them.

Garage and utility areas

Unheated spaces with water heaters or washer lines are common freeze spots.

Burst pipes are covered in more detail on our frozen pipe water damage page. For a split line inside a slab, see slab leak water damage.

Safety When a Pipe Bursts

Safety steps for a burst pipe

Shut off the water at the main only if it is safe and reachable. Keep people and pets out of standing water, and do not touch electrical items or breakers while standing in water.

Photograph the damage when it is safe. Call 911 for fire, gas, electrical danger or anyone in danger, and call the utility for a main-line problem.

Do not thaw a pipe with an open flame. Sewage or contaminated water needs professional handling, not fans or a household vacuum.

Extraction and Drying After a Burst Line

  1. Stop and repairThe water has to be off and the pipe fixed or capped before drying is worthwhile.
  2. ExtractPumps and extractors remove standing water from floors and carpet.
  3. MeasureMoisture meters find wet drywall, insulation and subfloor, including behind finished walls.
  4. DryAir movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach the dry goal, and materials that cannot dry are removed.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for this work, and you can ask any company whether it follows it. See water mitigation and emergency water removal for more.
