What Sets Each Town Apart

Water problems look different from one town to the next, because each city runs its own utility, drainage, and permit offices. These short notes come from each city's own published information.

Bedford

Bedford bills its own water and sewer, and its Public Works staff are on call around the clock. The city says every creek channel in town drains to the Trinity River and warns that debris can clog storm drain inlets in heavy rain. The full picture is on the Bedford page.

Euless

Euless gets its primary water supply from the Trinity River Authority and also runs city wells in the Trinity Aquifer. DFW Airport takes up about 3,228 of the city's 10,378 acres. Details are on the Euless page.

North Richland Hills

The city's own land use plan says roughly 45 percent of its single-family houses are 40 years old or older, which matters for supply lines and water heaters. It also publishes floodplain pages for residents. Read more on the North Richland Hills page.

Colleyville

Colleyville sits along Little Bear Creek and Big Bear Creek, and the city closes the Bedford Road crossing of Little Bear Creek when it floods. The city buys treated water from the Trinity River Authority. See the Colleyville page.

Haltom City

Haltom City's floodplain page names Big Fossil Creek, Little Fossil Creek, and the West Fork of the Trinity River, and the city holds a Class 8 rating in the National Flood Insurance Program. The sewer lateral belongs to the property owner under city policy. Start with the Haltom City page.

Picking the Right Town Page

Each town page lists the ZIP codes we serve there, the utility or city office to call for problems outside your walls, and the local conditions that shape drying and mold risk. If you share a ZIP with a neighbor town, read both pages, since the city that bills your water may not be the city on your mailing address.

Not sure which page fits? Call the dispatch line with your street address and we will tell you. If the water is clearly coming from the street or a city main, the utility is the right first call, and our crew handles what it left behind in your home.

What to Do Before a Crew Arrives

When water or mold is in the house, the first minutes matter. Use this short list while you call.

  • Stop the source if it is safe to do so, starting with the main water valve.
  • Do not touch electrical items or outlets near standing water.
  • Keep children and pets out of water that may be contaminated.
  • Take photos before moving anything.
  • Call 911 first for fire, gas smell, or a person in danger.
