Ceiling leaks

Water often shows up as a brown stain or a soft spot in the drywall. The real source may be in the attic, a roof penetration or a bathroom above the wall.

Supply-line leaks

Braided hoses at sinks, toilets and washers can split. Water spreads across one level and soaks baseboards, carpet and the bottom of the walls.

Water heater leaks

A tank that rusts through can release a lot of water into a closet or garage and wick into nearby flooring.

Drying a Single-Story House

A one-story house has no upper floor to hide a leak, but the attic above it can. Water in a ceiling often soaks insulation first, then drops into the room below. Water on the floor spreads across the whole level and creeps up into drywall by wicking.

Drying is planned by room and by material, not just by the visible wet spot. See structural drying for how that works and water damage cleanup for the first stage.

Extraction and Drying Step by Step

  1. Stop the sourceThe leak has to be shut off or repaired before drying has any lasting effect.
  2. Remove standing waterPumps and extractors pull water out of carpet, pad and low spots on the floor.
  3. Map the moistureMoisture meters check drywall, baseboard, subfloor and framing against a dry reference reading.
  4. Dry the structureAir movers and dehumidifiers run until the readings come down, and wet materials that cannot dry are cut out.
  5. Confirm and recordFinal readings are logged so you and your insurer can see the structure reached its dry goal.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration. You can ask any company whether its work follows it.

Water damage safety in your house

Keep people and pets out of standing or contaminated water. Do not touch electrical items or breakers while standing in water.

Shut off the water at the main only if it is safe and reachable. Photograph the damage when it is safe to do so.

Call 911 for fire, gas, electrical danger or anyone in danger. Call the utility for a main-line problem. Fans or a household vacuum are not enough for sewage or contaminated water.
