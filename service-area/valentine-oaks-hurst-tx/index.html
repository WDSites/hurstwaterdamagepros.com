Drain and Sewer Backups

Water that comes up through a floor drain, tub or toilet can carry contamination. The IICRC S500 standard treats sewage as the most heavily contaminated category of water. That is why porous materials like carpet and pad that touch it are often removed rather than cleaned.

Do not try to dry sewage with fans or a household vacuum. Those tools spread contamination and do not remove it. Restoration professionals we arrange use pumps, extractors and cleaning methods suited to the water category.

Water damage safety first

Keep people and pets out of standing or contaminated water. Do not touch electrical items or breakers while you are standing in water.

Shut off the water at the main only if it is safe and you can reach it. Photograph the damage when it is safe to do so.

Call 911 for fire, gas, electrical danger or anyone in danger. For main-line problems, call the utility, which in Hurst is Public Works Dispatch at 817-788-7212.

Heavy Rain and Water at the Door

Storm water entering a house is not automatically the worst category. The IICRC position statement on weather events says the category must be based on conditions actually found at the loss. A technician looks at the water, the materials and how long they were wet.

The City suggests pointing downspouts onto the lawn, away from paved areas. Its storm drainage division maintains about 14 miles of channels and hands out 20 sandbags per address on request. You place them yourself.

How Removal and Drying Work

  1. Remove the waterPumps and extractors pull standing water out before it spreads into walls.
  2. Measure the damageMoisture meters check drywall, baseboards and subfloor.
  3. Set the drying equipmentAir movers and dehumidifiers go in, and readings are repeated over the following days.
  4. Remove what cannot be savedContaminated pad, soaked insulation and swollen board come out, with the scope explained first.

ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published standard for professional water damage restoration. Our water mitigation page describes how mitigation fits ahead of any rebuild.

Insurance and Sewer Backup Coverage

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says sudden, accidental damage is usually covered, while gradual leaks and floods usually are not. TDI also lists sewer or drain backup as an optional endorsement on many policies. Ask your agent whether yours includes it.

Take photos before anything is removed, and keep moisture readings and an itemized scope. We cannot promise coverage. Scope and price are explained before work starts.
