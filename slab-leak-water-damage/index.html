A slab leak is a pipe leaking underneath the concrete floor of the house. You cannot see it, so the first clues are usually a floor that feels warm, carpet that stays damp at the edges, or a water bill that climbs. If water is coming up into the house, call (817) 623-9267 at any hour, because the line is answered 24/7.

What a slab leak is and where we fit

Water supply lines and drain lines run through or under the concrete slab in many slab-on-grade homes. When one of those lines splits, the water has nowhere to go but up through cracks, joints and the edges of the slab. It then soaks flooring, baseboards and the bottom of the walls.

We need to be plain about our role. We do not detect or repair lines under a slab. A leak detection plumber locates and fixes the line, and a foundation specialist deals with any settlement. We dry the finishes and structure above the slab, then rebuild what the water ruined.

The published standard for water damage work is ANSI/IICRC S500, and it assumes the owner finds and corrects the source of the water. That is why the plumber comes first. Drying a floor while the line keeps leaking only starts the job over.

The meter test you can do yourself

Turn off every faucet, appliance and irrigation zone, then look at your water meter. Most meters have a small leak indicator or a low-flow dial that spins when any water moves. If it keeps moving, water is going somewhere.

Next, close the main valve at the house and check again. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the home. If it keeps moving, the leak is on the line between the meter and the house. Tell the plumber what you saw, because it shortens the search.

Safety in the first hour: what to do before a crew arrives

Close the main water valve for the house, and stay out of rooms where water is near outlets or the panel. Cut power to a wet room only if you can reach the breaker without standing in water. A hot water line can leave a floor warm enough to hurt bare feet.

Call 911 for fire, a gas smell or anyone in danger. Call the City of Hurst Public Works dispatch line at 817-788-7212 only when the problem is on the city side of the meter. Then call a plumber who does leak detection, and call us for the drying.

Why slabs leak in North Texas

A Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation bulletin on foundations says expansive soils shrink and swell with moisture. Wet soil can lift a building and dry soil can let it settle, and either can cause serious damage. For a homeowner, that means soil movement is one of several things a plumber and a foundation specialist will consider when a slab line fails.

The USDA Natural Resources Conservation Service adds that expansive clay damages homes built without special precautions. It also quotes an American Society of Civil Engineers estimate that these soils cause more home damage each year than floods, tornadoes and hurricanes combined. That is a national figure, not a Tarrant County one, and it does not prove soil caused any particular leak. Corrosion and age also break pipes.

How we dry above the slab

  1. Wait for the water to be shut offThe plumber isolates or repairs the line, or at least the main is closed, so the floor stops refilling.
  2. Extract surface waterExtraction units remove standing water and wet carpet pad. Wet pad is usually discarded.
  3. Measure moistureMoisture meters check flooring, baseboards and lower walls. Thermal imaging shows temperature differences on a surface that can point to wet material, and the meter confirms it.
  4. Lift or open what traps waterCarpet may be lifted, baseboards removed and drywall opened along the bottom so air can reach the cavity.
  5. Dry the structureAir movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match unaffected areas. Antimicrobial treatment is added when the water category calls for it.

Slab water often comes from a supply line and starts clean, but it picks up dirt as it travels through soil and concrete. Drain line leaks are different and may be contaminated. Tell us which kind of line failed when you call.

Insurance and the city water bill

The Texas Department of Insurance says most policies cover sudden and accidental water damage but not continuous leaks. Its home insurance guide also lists damage to foundations or slabs as an optional endorsement. Ask your agent whether you have one, and check your policy before assuming anything. Report hidden water damage promptly after you first see it.

The City of Hurst says water customers can request a leak adjustment credit after a line break or equipment failure raises the bill. Credits are based on water consumption, and you submit repair receipts with the form. You stay responsible for the bill while it is reviewed. The city is installing advanced metering, but its notice does not promise leak alerts, so keep watching your own meter.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Do not ignore a warm floor or an unexplained bill increase
  • Do not cover damp flooring with new flooring or rugs
  • Do not drill into the slab yourself
  • Do not let a wet floor sit while you wait for estimates
  • Keep sprinkler lines and downspouts away from the foundation, as the NRCS advises
