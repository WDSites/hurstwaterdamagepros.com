Emergency Storm Water Damage in Hurst, TX

Hail, wind and driving rain can open a house in several places at once. We pull out the water and dry what got wet, after your roofer or window crew has the building closed up. Rising water is a different job, covered under flood damage restoration.

A strong thunderstorm does not leak in one spot. Hail bruises shingles, wind lifts flashing, a limb cracks a window, and rain finds every gap while the power is out. Storm water damage cleanup means getting that water out of the house fast and drying the ceilings, walls, insulation and floors it reached.

How Storms Put Water Inside a Hurst House

National Weather Service Fort Worth says large hail, damaging winds, flooding and tornadoes occur nearly every year somewhere in the Dallas-Fort Worth area, and that severe weather peaks in spring. For a Hurst homeowner, that means the roof, windows and siding are tested most in spring. A house that has come through a few storms can have small openings you cannot see from the ground.

The size of hail matters. On April 3, 2012, NWS staff at DFW Airport watched quarter-size hail fall while baseball-size hail landed about 100 yards away. Your neighbor's roof can look fine while yours is punctured. Check the attic after any big hail event.

First hour safety after a storm: what to do before a crew arrives

Call 911 for fire, a gas smell, downed lines or anyone hurt. Leave the house if you smell gas.

If water is coming through a ceiling near lights or outlets, stay out of that room and turn off the breaker for it only if you can reach the panel while dry. If you cannot, call us and wait outside the wet area.

Do not walk through standing water if any part of the house has lost power or a line is down. Move valuables up, take photos from a safe spot, and keep children and pets away from broken glass.

If water is coming from the city side of your meter or from the street, call the City of Hurst 24/7 Public Works Dispatch at 817-788-7212.

Rain Intrusion Versus Flood Water

Water from a storm is not automatically dirty. The IICRC, the group that publishes the water damage standard, said in a 2026 position statement that wind-driven rain and water entering from above is not automatically Category 3, the most contaminated class. The category depends on what crews actually find at your house. Rain through a roof into clean attic insulation is handled very differently from water that came across a yard and into the garage.

That sorting step drives the plan. Cleaner rain intrusion can often be dried in place. Water that picked up dirt, sewage or yard runoff means more removal and antimicrobial cleaning, and the flood damage approach applies.

How We Dry a Storm-Damaged House

  1. Find every wet areaMoisture meters check ceilings, walls and floors, since storm water often travels beyond the visible stain.
  2. Extract standing waterTruck-mounted or portable extraction units remove water from carpet, hard floors and low spots.
  3. Remove what cannot be savedSoaked ceiling drywall, wet insulation and carpet pad come out so they stop feeding moisture back into the room.
  4. Dry the structureAir movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match a dry reference, with daily checks and equipment moved as needed.
  5. Clean and documentSurfaces are cleaned, antimicrobial is used where the water category calls for it, and your readings and photos are kept for your insurer.

Drying follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for professional water damage restoration. Ask any company whether its crews work to it. We do not repair roofs, windows or siding. A licensed roofer or window contractor closes the openings, and we dry and clean the inside, then rebuild finishes through water damage repair.

What Changes the Cost

  • Number of openings: one punctured area is a smaller job than water entering through the roof, a window and a wall.
  • Water category and how long it sat before drying began.
  • Square footage and materials affected, such as hardwood or insulation.
  • Drying days and how many air movers and dehumidifiers the structure needs.
  • How much finish work comes after the drying is done.

Wind, Hail and Flood Are Different Policy Questions

The Texas Department of Insurance says windstorm is a covered loss for most policies, with an exception for coastal homes, and that a higher deductible can lower the premium. So ask your insurer what your wind and hail deductible is before a storm, not after. Flood damage is excluded from standard home policies and needs separate flood coverage.

Take photos or video before repairs, keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, and make temporary repairs only. Tell your insurer promptly. Always check your policy and ask your insurer what applies to your loss.

Mistakes to Avoid After a Storm

  • Waiting for the adjuster before any drying starts. Mold can begin in about a day, so dry quickly.
  • Climbing onto a wet or damaged roof to check it yourself.
  • Turning on ceiling fans or lights under a wet ceiling.
  • Driving through water on a road. NOAA says six inches of fast-moving water can knock a person off their feet, so turn around, do not drown.

Local Help Before and After a Storm

The City of Hurst says it delivers 20 sandbags per address for temporary emergency use on request through the Public Works line, 817-788-7212. You place and dispose of them yourself, so ask early. The city also clears debris from about 14 miles of open-lined channels after rainstorms, which helps street drainage but does not protect a single yard.

The National Weather Service tornado database for North and Central Texas shows April as the peak month, with 555 tornadoes between 1950 and 2025. That does not predict your street, but it makes spring the season to photograph your roof and find your breaker panel.
