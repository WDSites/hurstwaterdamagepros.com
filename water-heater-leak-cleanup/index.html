First-hour safety steps

Safety around a leaking water heater

If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility from outside. Do not flip switches or use a phone indoors. Otherwise, shut off power to an electric heater at the panel only if you can reach it without stepping in water, or turn a gas heater's control to off or pilot if it is safe to reach.

Close the cold supply valve on the pipe above the heater. Call the City of Hurst Public Works Dispatch at 817-788-7212 only for a problem on the city side of the meter, such as a main break. Water is not the only hazard, because the tank water can be scalding hot.

Why water heaters fail

Most leaks come from one of four places. The tank itself can corrode through, a supply line or fitting can loosen, the drain valve at the bottom can weep, or the temperature and pressure relief valve can discharge when pressure builds. Each one puts water in a different spot, so where the puddle sits tells you something.

Hurst water comes from a mix of surface water and Trinity Aquifer groundwater, with the City of Fort Worth as the primary supplier, according to the city's water quality report. The same report lists total hardness of about 108 to 150 parts per million. Moderately hard water leaves mineral sediment in a tank over time, so an older heater that has never been flushed is more likely to fail. Ask your plumber about flushing a newer one.

Where the water goes

In a garage the water runs to the lowest point and under the wall into the house. In a closet it soaks the baseboards and the carpet next door, and from an attic it comes through the ceiling below. Each setup has a different drying plan.

The leak is usually clean water at first, which the IICRC S500 water categories call Category 1. That changes the longer it sits. Sediment from the tank, wet carpet pad and old debris on a garage floor can add contamination, and the longer the water stays the more likely the job moves up a category. This is one reason to call early. Wet carpet next to the closet is covered on the carpet water damage page.

How the cleanup works

  1. Extract the waterExtraction units and wet vacuums pull water from the floor, carpet and pad.
  2. Find where it spreadMoisture meters check baseboards, drywall and subfloor. A thermal imaging camera shows cool wet areas, and the meter confirms them.
  3. Remove what cannot be savedSwollen baseboard, soaked drywall at the bottom of a wall and wet pad are cut out and bagged.
  4. Dry the structureAir movers and dehumidifiers run until readings match dry reference levels. See structural drying for the process in detail.
  5. Clean and rebuildSurfaces are cleaned, antimicrobial is applied where the water category calls for it, and finishes are replaced.

Replacing a water heater is plumbing work. Texas law, under Occupations Code Chapter 1301, treats plumbing repair and installation as licensed work, with an exception for an owner working on their own homestead. We do not repair or install heaters, so call a licensed plumber for that part. Hurst Building Inspections lists water heater inspections among its inspection types, so ask the city, at 817-788-7088, whether your replacement needs a permit.

What changes the cost

  • How much water spread and which rooms it reached.
  • Whether the water stayed clean or picked up contamination.
  • Materials involved, such as carpet, drywall, laminate or garage concrete.
  • Drying days and the equipment needed.
  • Rebuild scope once wet material has been removed.

Insurance and water heater leaks

Texas Department of Insurance guidance says sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a broken washer hose, is often covered, while gradual leaks and seepage usually are not. A heater that split open overnight is not the same as a slow drip over many months. Check your policy and ask your insurer, because the cost of the heater itself may be handled differently from the water damage.

Report the loss as soon as possible, take photos or video before repairs and keep the old tank or parts until the adjuster has seen them. Make only temporary repairs and keep receipts.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not wait to see if a drip stops. A slow leak behind a closet wall can soak the framing for weeks and may lead to mold, which is handled under mold remediation. Do not drain a full tank onto a finished floor, and do not relight a pilot in a wet area.

Work follows ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration, and you can ask any company whether its crews use it. Neighbors in Haltom City and Bedford call us for the same kind of leak.
